What it does

Relations as anchors. A feature anchor must be the only feature within 6 ppm over a 60 s window in every run; that fails wherever m/z is crowded, and it needs picked peaks. A confident scan-graph relation is keyed by its rule and the m/z of both ions, so it is far more specific, and it exists before peak detection. Relations are matched across runs by chain clustering on rule, anchor m/z, partner m/z and RT, at most one per run, in at least 60 % of runs. A relation is kept as an anchor only if:

  • it elutes: at least 60 % of its ions' signal within ±3σ lies within ±σ of its centre (σ = 2 elution sd). A relation between chemical-background ions has no localised elution and an arbitrary centre.
  • it has no competitor: no other ion within 6 ppm of either of its ions reaches 20 % of its height in the matching window. That other ion can be another trace, or the same trace beyond a valley, as with an isomer joined into one trace. Otherwise, when one of an isomer pair is missed in some runs, the other is matched in its place.
  • chance is negligible: its two ions' noise-floor windows are quiet enough that a chance relation of three consecutive scans is implausible.
  • it is unique in its run (no other confident relation with the same key in the window) and has SNR ≥ 5.

The matching window is not fixed. A first pass at ±60 s measures the drift (2.5 × the 95th percentile of anchor deviations, at least 10 s), and the filters are evaluated for that window. In dense Astral proteomics MS1 a stronger ion within 6 ppm is almost always present somewhere in ±60 s.

Centres. The relation says which two ions and roughly when. Its centre comes from both ions' full traces in a fixed window of ±2 elution sd (the run's own, from the scan graph), weighted by intensity² and re-centred. The scans where both ions were seen are not used directly. A weak partner is seen only near the apex, so that window is truncated by an amount that depends on abundance, and its centre has twice the error.

Precision, learned. SNR is height over the noise level in the ions' own m/z windows. The standard error is √((k₁·sd/SNR)² + (k₂·sd)²). k₁, k₂ are fitted on double differences: for two relations of one compound with no shared ion, (ta − tb) in run j minus the same in run j′. Drift, the compound's own RT error and each relation's bias cancel, so only measurement noise is left. A within-run estimate overstates the error about twofold, because truncation differs between relations.

Duplicates. Relations sharing an ion measure the same elution: an ion with an isotope, an adduct and a dimer partner anchors three times. Keeping one anchor per such group, or averaging the group, was tried and is worse. Averaging lets one wrong link merge two compounds' centres. Keeping one leaves some drift classes too sparse in places, and their warps wander there. With K and λ chosen by cross-validation instead of BIC, duplicates no longer inflate the number of classes, so all of them are kept.

Choosing K and λ. The drift penalty is not scale-free. The data term grows with the number and precision of anchors, so λ = 1 suits one anchor set and over-smooths another. BIC also over-counts anchors that share a compound's RT error. Both are therefore chosen by blocked cross-validation: anchors are assigned to 10 s RT blocks, so co-eluting siblings are held out together. Each held-out anchor's RT in every run is predicted from its other runs.

Noise per ROI

Whether a match is a coincidence depends on how busy the window it was accepted in is. Noise is therefore measured for every ROI in its own m/z window: the noise-floor acceptance width of the relation search, or the ROI's own scatter if wider, over its own scans. Centroids of the ROI itself are excluded. The map gives the per-scan chance of a spurious partner (hit fraction) and the local noise level (log-intensity mean and sd of interfering centroids). Relation SNR and the chance-relation filter use it. A wide tile averages a window next to a persistent background ion with an empty one; on real data the two disagree by orders of magnitude in both directions.

Against ground truth

Alignment batches as on the alignment page, same seeds. Held-out RT error: for every eluting compound and run, its true RT is predicted from its true RTs in the other runs with the fitted warps and class posterior. This scores the warps themselves, independent of which anchors they were learned from; the floor is the compound-level RT jitter (0.25 s · √(1 + 1/(J−1)) ≈ 0.27 s). F1 and aligned RT spread come from correspondence of picked features with that drift model and no refit, so only the anchors differ.

Mixed-species design (ProteoBench M7)

Conditions A and B mix human, yeast and E. coli digests at A/B = 1, 2 and 0.25 (log2 0, +1, −2; ProteoBench's scoring configuration). With no identifications, the A/B ratio of every cross-run group should still fall on one of three modes. A mixture with a common shift, one Gaussian per species at the known spacing and a uniform background scores grouping and quantification together: narrow modes mean precise ratios, background weight means groups that fit no species. free_mean lets each mode move on its own, which checks the design.

Relation groups are cross-run groups formed at the scan-graph stage, with no peak detection. 550,296 relation groups are formed by rule and the m/z of both ions in reference time (drift from relation anchors; grouping gap 22.1 s). Each is quantified per run by the sum of its two ions' maxima in the fixed window. Feature groups are picked peaks matched by the current pipeline, and again with the relation drift. Groups need two values in each condition and are stratified by median SNR.

Next steps

  • Correspondence of graph nodes. Here the drift model is used to match picked features. Next comes matching scan-graph nodes (trace blocks with centres from the same fixed-window estimator) across runs under this model, neighbourhood-aware, so isomers at one m/z do not swap.
  • Cross-run edges with dropout. Lift relations to node-group pairs. Re-query runs where a relation is missing at its predicted place in raw centroids, and score presence and absence against detectability, using the per-ROI noise level. The chance rate is the RT-shifted decoy hit rate in the ions' own windows.
  • Sample design. A runs × sources matrix (replicates, spikes, matrix, blank, dilution) for expected presence and intensity ratios.
  • Vector-coherent partition. GAEC with one centre per run (Ward increment summed over runs, missing runs at zero precision), so ions that move together across runs group even without a mass rule.
