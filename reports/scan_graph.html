Feature level vs scan level

The feature-level graph (annotation) relates picked peaks, so it inherits every miss of peak detection and judges co-elution from apex times. Here the modulo test runs between centroids in every scan: each centroid of a trace is an anchor, the best partner for every rule target (n·Δ/z, or a neutral-mass map for charge states and multimers) is searched in the same scan and, when absent, in the neighbouring scans (±1 scan bridging for partners near the noise floor). Partners need not belong to a trace, so isotopes too weak to form one are rescued. Overlapping ~2 Da isotope targets are merged into one family so every M+2 pair is tested once.

Relations over time. A real relation persists across the anchor's elution: one consistent partner, a residual whose inverse-variance mean shrinks as 1/√n, a constant intensity ratio, co-varying intensities. Instances are cut into segments of consecutive supporting scans and summarised by those statistics. The same search is run with decoy rules; a weighted logistic model of targets vs decoys gives each segment a posterior (target-decoy local FDR). A second pass adds triangle support: the number of third nodes confidently related to both ends of an edge, since real relations form dense structures (M, M+1, M+2, adducts and their isotopes) and coincidences rarely close triangles.

Compounds in graph space. Traces are cut into blocks covering the time ranges of their relations (isomers sharing an m/z stay apart), blocks become nodes, segments become edges weighted by log-odds relative to a merge threshold, and correlation clustering yields compounds with a time extent and a summed compound chromatogram. Peak detection, if wanted, then runs on that chromatogram, where every member ion adds signal. An elution-time prior keeps each compound's members within a peak width of each other.

Accuracy against ground truth

A compound counts as found when one group joins at least two of its ions and that compound is the group's plurality, so oversized merged groups do not count. Relation precision is the fraction of relations at posterior ≥ 0.9 linking two ions of one compound.

Elution-time prior

Relations are local in time: a segment says two ions are related at these scans. Partitioning on relations alone therefore lets a long-lived ion act as a hub: chemical background spans minutes, and one coincidental relation during a peak's elution attaches that peak to it, so a compound ends up holding members whose signal sits minutes apart. Ions of one compound elute together, so the partition carries a prior at the run's own peak width:

  • Peak width from the data. Every node gets the intensity-weighted centre and spread of its signal. The typical spread σ is the median over nodes of confident relations, and the peak width is its FWHM (2.355 σ, a moment width, so tails count).
  • Bulk containment. The bulk of each node's signal must lie within one peak width of the relation linking it. An edge is discounted by log fa + log fb, where f is the share of the node's signal inside that window. A 10-second relation explains a peak but not a plateau that lasts minutes.
  • Co-elution. Member signal centres scatter around the compound's centre by a learned τ plus each centre's own standard error (spread / √neff), so peaks near the noise floor need not apex together. Inside GAEC merging two clusters costs the Ward increment ½·PAPB/(PA+PB)·Δcentre², and chains of individually good links can no longer reach across the run. τ is fitted by a Gaussian + uniform mixture to centre differences of confident, contained relations.

The prior acts only on the partition. Relation posteriors and their FDR are unchanged. On synthetic data long-lived background ions are deliberately no longer joined across time, which is why the background ARI drops while eluting compounds group as well or better.

Learned per run: σ = typical intensity-weighted elution spread (s), fwhm = 2.355 σ, τ = centre scatter of co-eluting members beyond their standard errors (s), inlier = share of confident relations in the Gaussian component. Limitations

  • With the elution-time prior, group purity is close to the feature graph's. The remaining impurity comes from coincidental relations between ions that really do co-elute. Negative evidence between co-eluting blocks with poor ratio constancy and an isotope-envelope constraint per block would address it.
  • Long-lived background ions are split into time-local pieces, one per stretch of relations. When background matters (e.g. for subtraction) its pieces could be re-joined by trace identity in a separate step.
  • Relations are anchored on the lighter ion; a relation whose lighter partner is too weak to form a trace is only found if the heavier ion's reverse search is added.
  • Posteriors are mildly overconfident mid-range (≈0.1) and well calibrated at the extremes.
