Traces (ROI detection)

Multi-target tracking of centroids across scans: each trace keeps an inverse-variance m/z estimate, links are scored by an intensity-dependent precision model that is learned from the data, conflicts are solved exactly by linear assignment, and short dropouts are re-joined.

What it does

More than an m/z slice. An m/z slice (fixed bins) or a greedy ROI builder (join the nearest open ROI within a fixed ppm window) treats every centroid alike. The tracker instead predicts where each active trace will be in the next scan and how certain that prediction is: a trace's m/z is an inverse-variance mean of its centroids, with exponential forgetting so slow calibration drift is followed, and each centroid's own m/z uncertainty comes from a precision model σ(I) = sqrt(a² + (b·I^-c)²). Linking costs are negative log-likelihoods (m/z residual, intensity continuity, gap length); a link is accepted only if it beats the cost of starting a new trace, and competing links are resolved by the Hungarian algorithm on each conflict component (the frame-to-frame step of u-track-style particle tracking). A second assignment pass joins trace ends to trace starts across short dropouts.

Self-calibration. After a first pass the precision model is re-fitted by maximum likelihood on differences between consecutive centroids of the same trace. Differences cancel slow drift, so the fit isolates scan-to-scan precision; tracking then runs again with the calibrated model. Compared with the original webtrace (iterative min-cuts on a k-NN graph), conflicts are solved optimally per scan and the cost has a probabilistic meaning.

Learned precision model

The m/z precision of a centroid as a function of its intensity, estimated without ground truth from consecutive centroids of the same trace. On synthetic data it recovers the simulator's model; on Astral data it finds an Orbitrap-like ~1/SNR law with a sub-ppm floor.

On real low-load (250 pg) Astral data the tracker places about half of all centroids in traces, versus roughly a third for the greedy ROI builder, with a wider within-trace m/z spread. This is the calibrated model admitting low-intensity centroids, whose m/z genuinely is imprecise (σ grows as 1/SNR). Synthetic data shows the cost: slightly more noise centroids end up in traces (noise_in_traces column) in exchange for much higher completeness. Traces are deliberately permissive; peak detection, which requires shape and signal-to-noise, is the filter.
