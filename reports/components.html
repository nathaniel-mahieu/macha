Components: peaks of relation clusters

A component is one compound's elution in one run: the ions its confident scan-graph relations join, fitted with one shared exponentially modified Gaussian. Components split where two sources share an ion, are linked across runs through relation groups, and are quantified in every run with their learned shape.

What a component is

Shared shape. The ions joined by a compound's confident relations are fitted together: yi(t) = ai·f(t; μ, σ, τ) + bi, with f an exponentially modified Gaussian shared by all ions, one amplitude per ion and a constant baseline per ion (real traces sit on a chemical-background level). Amplitudes and baselines are solved in closed form by non-negative least squares (variable projection); the shape is fitted by Levenberg-Marquardt. Every partner trace is a separate noisy view of the same elution, so all of them pin the shape. The intensity variance is noisei² + (cv·model)². The noise level is measured in each ion's own m/z window, and cv is learned from high-intensity residuals. A scan in the window where an ion has no centroid is censored at that noise level: it costs only where the model rises above it. This is the dropout floor.

Splits. A second component (own apex, shared width, its own amplitude per ion) is kept when it improves the fit by more than a BIC-style penalty. Most ions then belong to one side: a partner trace that peaks with one source becomes a template for it. A component whose one-component fit runs into the width bounds or out of its window has no bounded peak (chemical background, or a weak cluster at the noise level): it is flagged as a plateau and never split. Two sources closer than 0.75 sd are detected but not separable (their amplitude ratio and source m/z trade off), so they stay one component, flagged merged.

Merged traces. Ions closer than about one resolution FWHM (several ppm) are not resolved, and the instrument reports their intensity-weighted mean m/z. With two sources in one ion, the centroid m/z follows m(t) = m2 + (m1 − m2)·g(t) + β·log I(t), where g(t) is source 1's share of the signal at time t. It moves from one source to the other as dominance changes. An intensity-dependent shift of a single ion (the β term) gives the same m/z at the same intensity on both flanks; two sources trace a loop. Centroid precision (~0.3 ppm at high intensity) is far below the resolution limit, so these m/z residuals enter the same objective, weighted by the learned precision model. The m/z difference of the two sources has a Gaussian prior scaled by the coalescence limit, estimated from the run's own closest within-scan centroid spacing.

Across runs. Each relation group (one relation matched across runs in reference time) votes for the components its members belong to. Mutual best-voted components of different runs are linked, keeping one per run. Pieces found in disjoint sets of runs with the same ions and matching reference time are joined. Relation groups break where a compound's drift class departs from the mean warp, and the pieces then cover complementary runs. Each cross-run component is then quantified in every run with its median shape and ion ratios: within ±0.5 sd of its fitted apex where the run's own fit found it, elsewhere at the apex predicted from the runs that did (drift class inferred from them), searched under a Gaussian prior. Quantification uses the two ions of the component's strongest relation group. A relation cluster in dense data can hold several co-eluting analytes, and a confident relation's two ions certainly belong together. Each ion's chromatogram takes the closest centroid per scan within the tracker's intensity-dependent tolerance, over a refitted baseline, censored at the ion's own noise.

Presence. Every value comes with a likelihood ratio: χ² of the baselines alone minus χ² with the component. Where the component is absent, its amplitude measures noise and interference, which compresses ratios between runs towards 1. Ratios therefore use the runs with LLR ≥ 25 (about 5 sd); "every run" shows what happens without that.

Deconvolution on ground truth

One synthetic run with 120 near-isobaric pairs placed 0.5-6 ppm apart: closer than the resolution, so co-present ions coalesce into one centroid. They elute within 2.5 peak sd of each other, with heights up to 10× apart. A pair counts as resolved when two different components (or picked peaks) on its main ion each have an apex within 0.5 peak sd of one of the true apexes.
