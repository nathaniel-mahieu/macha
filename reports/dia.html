What is compared

A DIA isolation window visited once per cycle is an LC-MS run of fragment ions, so each window's MS2 time series goes through trace detection unchanged. Five groupings then assemble fragment traces into pseudo-spectra:

  • co-elution only: correlation clustering of traces whose apexes coincide, on elution-profile correlation (DIA-Umpire-style pseudo-spectra without MS1);
  • ladder graph: the scan-level graph with amino-acid residue steps as the modulo rules (plus fragment 13C, H2O / NH3 / CO losses and 1+/2+ pairs). Its residue decoys sit on the peptide mass-defect line, more than 0.02 Da from every real step, each keeping one residue's offset from the line, because unrelated fragment pairs also differ by peptide-like masses;
  • ladder-seeded: ladder components (near-pure, but one per ion series) merged when their summed chromatograms co-elute and correlate, then grown by every trace that follows a seed's profile (shared);
  • MS1-anchored: the classic precursor-centric route: MS1 features in the window, charge from isotope spacing, and the fragments whose profiles follow each precursor's MS1 profile (shared between precursors);
  • MS1-anchored + ladder rescue: the MS1-anchored groups plus those ladder-seeded groups whose fragments no MS1 precursor claimed, i.e. precursors MS1 missed, searched together under one FDR.

All groups go through one search (macha.search): tryptic human + yeast peptides (1 missed cleavage, 7-30 residues) and pseudo-reversed decoys whose precursor fits the window at charge 2-4, b / y fragment matching at 10 ppm, and a binomial score against the chance-match rate implied by the group's own fragment density. Groups without MS1 get a precursor mass from complementary b / y pairs (bi + yL-i = M + 2H+) when that pile-up beats the same search in wrong windows. Identifications are counted at 1 % peptide-level FDR (target-decoy competition). On synthetic data the database is the human + yeast digest plus the simulated peptides, so any accepted human or yeast peptide is a known false identification and the actual FDR is measured. The search is deliberately simple (no spectral library, intensity or retention-time prediction), so absolute numbers are far below DIA-NN-class tools; it is the same for every grouping, which is what the comparison needs.

What the ladders buy

  • MS1 is the strongest anchor when the precursor is visible. A precursor mass and a smooth, high-signal elution template make MS1-anchored groups the most identifiable: 3963 / 3473 peptides on the synthetic 2 / 20 Th runs and 423 on the Astral windows.
  • Without MS1, ladders beat co-elution where windows are crowded. Ladder-seeded groups identify 1717 vs 1444 peptides at 20 Th and 318 vs 229 on the Astral data, where co-isolated precursors make pure co-elution groups chimeric. In narrow 2 Th windows co-elution is already clean (2601 vs 2384).
  • Ladders alone are too sparse. A ladder component only holds fragments whose neighbouring ion is also observed, and a precursor's b and y series are separate components, so the bare ladder graph identifies little; its value is as a seed.
  • As a rescue for precursors MS1 misses, ladders add a little. Adding only ladder-seeded groups that no MS1 precursor claimed gives 438 vs 423 on the Astral windows (3462 vs 3473 and 4114 vs 3963 on synthetic). Adding all ladder groups instead lowered identifications in a first run: every extra group is another chance for a decoy.
